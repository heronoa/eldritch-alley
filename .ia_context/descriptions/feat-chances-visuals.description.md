### 1. What this MR delivers

**The board now answers "what is under the mouse" by drawing it.** Hovering the pointer over a tile outlines that tile's top face in the identity's paper white; hovering the *figure* of a unit outlines the tile that unit stands on, and hovering a unit's portrait in the turn queue outlines that unit's tile while the pointer is nowhere near the board — the three readings a click already resolves, in the order a click resolves them (EA-8), so the tile marked is the tile a press there would act on. Nothing of it reaches the server: it is a `Graphics` the scene draws and takes down, exactly like the existing highlights, and it holds no rule.

**It is a second, independent mark, not a repurposing of the hit-chance preview.** The plan's D1 kept a separate `hoveredCell` rather than widening EA-18's `previewCell`, so the preview's own mode gate (`attack`/`ability`) is untouched and the outline and the odds badge are drawn by two questions that never overwrite each other — the move/attack wash, the hit-chance numbers and the outline can all be on one tile at once, which is the whole point of the owner's request. Two consequences follow from that choice and are the owner's (D3, D5): the mark keeps working while the *bot* plays and while a replay's camera swings, because it answers "what is under the mouse" and not "what could I do now"; and it is cleared the moment the match is decided, because the result screen and the battle report are read against no board at all.

**The two badges over a unit's head are now named and backed.** `Acerto 62%` / `Hit 62%` replaces a bare `62%`, and `Cobertura: leste` / `Cover: East` replaces the full sentence `Em cobertura a leste` / `In cover for East` — a playtester reading a number with no noun over a unit had to guess what it was answering. The Portuguese keeps the fiction's own word (`cobertura`) rather than naming the effect, and the label form is what lets the two badges be read together as a pair. Both badges also gained a **tag**: a miniature of the panel — its carbon fill, its frame, its own alpha — floated behind the words and resized to them on every change, so the text is read against a controlled backing instead of the tile underneath it. On the night sky the old badges read on nothing; over the lightest tile the maps draw, they read on a light background; both are now the same panel everywhere. That change moved the contrast question rather than answering it, so it brought its own cases (§2, `theme.contrast.test.ts`).

**Verification at the head of this branch:** `npm test -w @eldritch-alley/frontend` 841/841 in 61 files; `npm test -w @eldritch-alley/engine` 342/342 in 22 files; `npm test -w @eldritch-alley/game-server` 86/86 in 8 files; `npm run typecheck` and the build's own `tsc --noEmit` both clean; `npm run build -w @eldritch-alley/frontend` passes (entry 211.77 kB / gzip 67.99 kB, CSS 3.81 kB / gzip 1.43 kB, match chunk 1,612.15 kB / gzip 382.69 kB, with the same sub-500 kB chunk warning this project has carried since M2). Unlike the previous MR in this area, the feature is fully inside the automated suite's reach: the hover path is synchronous — pointer event in, mark out — with no timer to fire, so a Phaser stand-in that never runs a `delayedCall` proves nothing less here than a browser would for the drawing itself. What no test covers is the *look* of it: the paper outline on the three maps' own palettes, and the two tags against the busiest tile in each.

**Divergences from the approved plan: none in the hover layer**, which was implemented as §3 of the plan describes it, decisions D1–D5 included. The **badge pass has no plan at all** — it is the owner's request as it was made, applied to the two catalogs and to `UnitSprite`, with its reasoning written into the catalogs' own comments instead of a plan document; that is a gap in this branch's paper trail, named here rather than left for the reviewer to notice.

### 3. What this MR does not deliver

Both items were scoped out by the owner when the plan's questions were resolved on 2026-10-15 (plan §7), not by this branch's author, and both are recorded in the plan's §6 rather than dropped silently:

- **Fading the building that stands over the hovered tile.** `drawCoveredBuildings`' own comment has been saying since the cover work that the client has no hover to read a covered cell from — which is no longer true, so this is now a real follow-up rather than an impossible one. It is a change to how the map is drawn, not to what the mouse marks, and the owner left it out of this round. Tracked in the plan's §6.
- **Any finger equivalent of the hover.** The owner's request named the mouse and the portrait; a touch pointer reports no hover at all (EA-18's own rule, unchanged) and the long press remains the finger's answer to a different question. Nothing here gives a finger a hover, and nothing here takes the long press away.

### 4. Notes for the reviewer

- **Two subjects in one branch, plus a third small one.** Read it by commit: `4711a0b` is the badge wording and the tags, `ee58df0` is the tile hover (with the plan that goes with it), and the `README.md` tag bump rides inside the first of them. Against `CLAUDE.md` rule 5 that is a split worth making before this lands; the description covers both subjects because the branch does.
- **`hudCoversPoint` duplicates `hudTakesPress`'s rectangles and must be kept in step with it by hand (D4).** I read the two lists against each other at this head and they match, in the same order: the ability menu, the inspection window, the countdown (line and link), the settings gear, the settings panel, the log, the action bar's buttons, the camera panel, a pending move's chips, and every rectangle `hudRects()` lists. The alternative — splitting `hudTakesPress` into "is this point covered" and "act on this point" — was rejected on purpose, because that path carries roughly twenty tests and each of its branches does something on the way through. If you touch either function, touch both.
- **The extraction of `sameCell`/`cellUnderPointer` from `updatePreview` is a refactor carried by this feature, not part of it.** The behaviour of the preview is unchanged: `sameCell` answers what the inline comparison answered (null on either side means the two are equal only when both are), and the preview and the hover now resolve a pointer through the same function, which is what keeps the marked tile and the pressed tile the same tile. It is in the diff because the feature needed it, and it is the reason a hover case can assert against `LAYER.hover` without the preview interfering.
- **The test stand-in for `this.add` is unusual for this project** (a `Proxy` over the Phaser stub's own graphics that records `setDepth`/`strokePoints`/`fillPoints`/`destroy`). It exists because the stub has no real `Graphics` and the mark is nothing but drawing calls, so depth and stroke-vs-fill are the only observable facts. Every other `add` name is handed on untouched, so the sprites a case creates are still the stub's own.
- **No server, no engine, no protocol.** `git diff develop...feat/chances-visuals -- backend/` is empty; no new engine export, no new field on the wire, no new ADR. The whole diff is the client drawing what it already knows plus two catalog values — which is also why the game-server and engine suites were run only as a regression check.
- **The hover has no offset, zoom or rotation case of its own**, and doesn't need one: the mark is kept in board space (`mapCell`) and drawn back through `viewCell`, so it follows the tile through a rotation and through zoom rather than following the screen point. A case that rotated the camera *without moving the mouse* would be testing `pushHud`'s redraw rather than the hover, and `pushHud` is where the mark is redrawn for every mutation already.
